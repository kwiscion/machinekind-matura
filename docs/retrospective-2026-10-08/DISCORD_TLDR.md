**Matura hackathon retrospective**

We scored **46/60**, versus the winner's **54/60**. The experiment was also about autonomous agent teamwork—and that fell short of our expectations.

**Main finding:** agents could implement and verify bounded tasks, but research direction remained owner-dependent. Revisiting Qwen, increasing reasoning budgets, fixing image inputs, and reopening RAG required human intervention.

**Other failures:** checks often proved software mechanics rather than task suitability; some experiments could not resolve the intended uncertainty; preparation, integration and grading were not consistently budgeted through to a usable result. A broken experiment sometimes stood in for evidence against an idea.

**What worked:** bounded execution, recovery, preserving raw evidence and candidate answers, and independent reviews that actually blocked bad promotions or changed decisions.

**Agreed changes:**
• A dedicated expert owns research direction; a manager owns execution and feasibility.
• Strategic reassessment follows meaningful results and a regular cross-project review.
• Qualify the actual inputs and model behavior before scaling.
• Every experiment must connect a clear question to a decision.
• Manage the full delivery path, with explicit blocker, extension and stop decisions.

Keep **one compact project notebook**: roughly 300 words for current state and 75 per completed experiment; link raw artifacts instead of copying them.

The evidence record covers **38 cases plus 2 context records**. We did not establish that RAG caused the score improvement or what percentage of time was bureaucracy.
